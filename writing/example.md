# What Is a Data Center?

You've probably seen the headlines: a data center is being planned near someone's neighborhood, and the neighbors are not happy. Maybe you wouldn't want one near your home either. But what is it you'd be saying no to? Most of us have no idea what actually goes on inside one.

This post fixes that. By the end, you'll know what a data center is, what happens inside it, and why it needs so much power. Then you can judge for yourself.

## A Friday night story

It's 8 p.m. You sit down and press play on a movie. Your phone doesn't contain that movie. It sends a quick request out over the internet, like placing an online order: "One movie, please."

That order lands in a data center, which works like a giant warehouse that never closes, the kind that packs and ships online orders. Let's walk through it.

![A phone sends an order across the internet to a data center, which ships the movie back.](flow:Your phone|The internet|Data center|Your movie)

## The warehouse

A data center is a large building full of powerful computers with no screen or keyboard. They only do work for other people.

- **Computers are the workers.** They read each order and run the software that handles it.
- **Storage is the shelves.** Rows of drives hold the movie, your photos, and your emails.
- **The network is the conveyor belts and loading docks.** Cables carrying pulses of light connect everything to everything else, and to the outside world.

![Three parts of the warehouse: computers are the workers, storage is the shelves, and the network is the conveyor belts.](map:Computers=The workers|Storage=The shelves|The network=The conveyor belts)

At the door, a dispatcher sends your order to a worker who isn't busy, like a manager pointing you to the shortest line. Within a blink, the movie is pulled off the shelves and shipped to your phone. Thousands of other orders are being handled at the same moment.

## So what is "the cloud"?

You've heard the term. The cloud is not a place in the sky. It's a nickname for using someone else's computers over the internet instead of your own.

Why "cloud"? Engineers used to draw network diagrams and sketch the internet as a fluffy cloud, meaning "stuff out there we don't need to detail." The name stuck.

So how does it relate to a data center? Think of a stack of DVDs at home versus a movie warehouse across town. Keeping your own stack is like using your own computer. Ordering from the warehouse is "using the cloud." The data center is that warehouse, the real building you never see. "Cloud" is how it feels to you: things just show up. "Data center" is where it all physically lives.

## The warehouse never closes

A warehouse that loses power stops shipping. A data center can't afford that, so it plans for trouble:

- **Power.** Electricity comes from the power grid, like at your house, only far more of it. If the grid fails, giant batteries take over instantly, like a flashlight in a blackout. Then the building's own diesel generators start up and can run for days.
- **Spares.** Operators keep extra equipment on hand so one broken part never stops the shipping.

![The power grid feeds the computers normally. Batteries and generators are the two backups.](flow:Power grid|Batteries|Diesel generators)

## The heat problem

Think about your laptop getting warm on your lap. Now picture thousands of them, running nonstop in one building. Nearly all the electricity those computers use turns into heat, and hot chips slow down or break.

So a data center works like a warehouse with giant exhaust fans. Cold air is blown in at the front of the stacks of computers, and hot air is pulled out the back and carried outside. Some newer designs pipe liquid right up to the chips, because liquid carries heat away better than air.

![Cool air flows into the front of a stack of computers, and hot air is pulled out the back.](flow:Cool air in the front|Computers|Hot air out the back|Outside)

## The bottom line

A data center is a warehouse for information. Computers work the orders, drives hold the goods, cables carry them out, and the building's biggest jobs are keeping the power on and the heat out. Next time you press play, you'll know where your order is being filled.
